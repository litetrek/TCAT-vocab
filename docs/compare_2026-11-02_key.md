## Current English in T-CAT (for reference)

**1.** 1. First, we discuss the greater teaching and A lesser teaching, then examine the false teachings and the true.

**2.** The eighty-four thousand Dharma doors were imparted over the forty-nine years in which Buddha transmitted the Dharma in this world. Each Dharma was taught for the liberation of sentient beings, with an object of focus established to accord with their capacities. Such correspondence consists in the coming together of causes and conditions; subsequently, various divisions arose, including Hinayana, the Lesser Vehicle; Mahayana, the Greater Vehicle; and Vajrayāna (Tantric or Esoteric Vehicle).

**3.** The distinctions among the various Vehicles are established to accord with the causes and conditions of sentient beings, providing each with a corresponding realm of Dharma. Without the appropriate conditions, the Dharma cannot truly resonate with a person, so how could one gain the fruit of attainment? Therefore, those engaged in spiritual practice should follow their own conditions, adopt the Dharma suited to them, and advance in their cultivation accordingly. They must not force what does not accord with their conditions; otherwise, all their efforts will be in vain and yield nothing.

**4.** But worldly people carry habitual tendencies of greed and attachment that are without beginning. Because of these, they grasp at the superior Dharma for themselves, presumptuously craving the great while dismissing the lesser as trivial. In the Saha world, this happens again and again, plain to see, like a chronic ailment. Once this cause is planted, it often spirals beyond control. At the end of life, not only do they gain nothing at all, but they may even fall in suffering into the intermediate state (Bardo in Tibetan) and turn once more in Samsara (the cycle of rebirth). In the end, one who set out to learn and practice Buddhism becomes not a sage but a ghost!

**5.** Thus, such cases are all too common, for this course arises from grasping desire and is also a manifestation of ignorance.

**6.** Yet the desire for the Dharma, the pursuit of understanding, and the attainment of sagehood belong to pure causes.

**7.** Yet because the views one clings to are mistaken, one disparages A lesser teaching and thereby creates conditions conducive to conduct that is out of accord. This incurs two offenses: once their causes are sown, their fruits will inevitably be reaped.
